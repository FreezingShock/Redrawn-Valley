Valley map

Every page, where it happens

A hand-drawn map of the San Fernando Valley. Each study, field guide entry and note that has a place is pinned to it. Pages about the whole Valley are listed beside the map instead of being pinned to a spot they do not belong to.

How to read it

A schematic, with its limits stated

I drew the map in code from approximate coordinates. It is for finding things, not for measuring.

Pins

Brick pins are studies, moss pins are field guide entries and ink pins are notes. Two or more pins that are close together merge into a count; click it to zoom in.

Dashed rings

Each pin has a ring for how sure its location is: the site, the street (about a quarter mile) or the neighborhood (about a mile). Hover a pin to see its ring.

Valley-wide pages

A page on tree canopy or sidewalk law has no single place, so I list it as Valley-wide rather than invent a pin.

New pages appear by themselves

A page joins the map when its head carries an rv-geo tag. The build reads every page and rewrites the pin list.

What I could not verify

  • Rivers, freeways and the valley edge are placed by eye from public maps and are good to about a quarter mile. I did not survey them.
  • Pins marked neighborhood are the middle of the area a page is about, not an address. Pins close together on the same subject are nudged apart so both can be seen.
  • I have not tested the map on every phone. I checked it at 375 and 1280 pixels wide.
