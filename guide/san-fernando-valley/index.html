The setting#

The San Fernando Valley, “the Valley” locally, is an urbanized valley in Los Angeles County, north of the Los Angeles Basin. It covers about 260 square miles and had about 1.8 million people in 2023. It is bounded by the Santa Susana Mountains to the northwest, the Simi Hills to the west, the Santa Monica Mountains to the south, the Verdugo Mountains to the east and the San Gabriel Mountains to the northeast.[1] It includes a large part of the City of Los Angeles plus Burbank, Calabasas, Glendale, Hidden Hills, San Fernando and several unincorporated areas.[1]

Why it runs hot#

Because of its relatively inland position and other factors, summer days in the Valley are typically hotter, and winter nights colder, than in the Los Angeles Basin.[1] The extreme is Woodland Hills: on Sept. 6, 2020 the official site at Pierce College reached 121°F, the highest temperature ever recorded at an official station in Los Angeles County; it was reached at about 1:30 p.m.[2][3] That is a single day, and a record at one station, but it is the reason I picked this part of the city.

The mechanics of why built-up places stay hotter are in urban heat island.

Water and ground#

The Valley holds the headwaters and a long reach of the Los Angeles River, with tributaries such as the Tujunga Wash, and some of the clearest examples of retrofitting hard ground to take rain, such as the Elmer Avenue project in Sun Valley.[4]
