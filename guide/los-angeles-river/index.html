Where it starts#

The Los Angeles River’s official beginning is the confluence of two channelized streams, Bell Creek and Arroyo Calabasas, in the Canoga Park part of Los Angeles, just east of State Route 27, which is Topanga Canyon Boulevard.[1] From there it flows about 51 miles through the San Fernando Valley, downtown Los Angeles and the Gateway Cities to San Pedro Bay at Long Beach, draining a watershed of about 827 square miles.[1] My first study site is on that same boulevard, though I have not traced where its runoff goes.

The river in the Valley#

The Sepulveda Basin is a flood-control basin and, with the Griffith Park area and the Willow Avenue estuary in Long Beach, one of only three stretches with a soft bottom.[2] The Tujunga Wash is a 13-mile urban tributary that was channelized for flood control in the 1950s.[3]

Why it matters to a small place#

Most of the river is a concrete channel designed to move floodwater out quickly, which is the same logic as a storm drain on a parking lot (see impervious surface). The revitalization office reports that only the Sepulveda Basin and the Glendale Narrows presently support riparian habitat in the 32-mile project area.[4] A site that holds water and plants native trees is a small version of what the river lost (see riparian corridor).
