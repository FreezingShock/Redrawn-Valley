What it is#

A riparian corridor is the band of plants, soil and wildlife habitat beside a stream. Before the Los Angeles River was channelized, the areas next to it were riparian ecosystems with many native plant and animal species.[3]

What was lost#

The City of Los Angeles’s river revitalization office cites the California Coastal Conservancy: close to 100% of the original wetlands and 90–95% of in-stream riparian habitat in the Los Angeles River watershed have been lost to urbanization and channelization. Within the 32-mile river project area, the only places that currently support riparian habitat are the Sepulveda Basin and the Glendale Narrows, and both are stressed by exotic species, changed hydrology, dumped trash and encroaching development.[1]

Sepulveda Basin#

The Sepulveda Basin in the Valley is one of three places along the river with a soft bottom, and native willows dominate the banks between Burbank and Balboa Boulevards, though some non-native trees such as eucalyptus tower over the lower native shrubs.[4] The basin is also a flood-control basin, so the habitat and the engineering share the same ground.

Tujunga Wash Greenway#

A built example: the 1.2-mile Tujunga Wash Greenway turned an inaccessible right-of-way along a concrete flood channel into 15 acres of riparian habitat, 50 to 60 feet wide, planted with 1,100 trees and 18,000 shrubs and vines, all California natives. A naturalized streambed is fed by up to 325,000 gallons a day diverted from the channel. The project reports it infiltrates up to 118 million gallons of water a year from the channel, recharging the San Fernando groundwater basin, and cuts potential landscape water use by 70–80% by using native plants.[2]

These are the project team’s reported figures from a case study brief, not independent measurements.

Why a small site cares#

My studies are not on river banks. But the Valley’s streets and lots drain toward these channels, so what I do with rain on a parking lot is the first link of the same system. See bioswale and impervious surface.
