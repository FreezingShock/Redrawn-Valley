Method

Four steps, in this order

The order matters. The drawing comes second, and the proposal comes last.

1

Read the site

Walk it, photograph it at the hottest hour, and draw what is really there in plan: the building, the stalls, the driveways, the trees.

2

Find the evidence

Measured results only: government data, peer-reviewed studies and the city’s own code. Anything I cannot verify gets flagged.

3

Draw the fix

A plan, a section and a detail that fit the site as it is. Then count what it costs, like parking stalls given up.

4

Say what is unknown

Every study ends with its limits and with the numbers I would measure before and after to know whether it worked.

Notes

From the notebook

Short pieces on what each study taught me. More as the studies come.

The first plan I had for Topanga Willows was wrong. It showed the building at the back of the lot with parking in front, because that is what a strip center usually looks like.

The aerial photo says otherwise. The building sits right on the sidewalk and all the parking is behind it. That one fact changed the whole argument: the storefronts face west into the afternoon sun, and the building only shades the first row of cars.

Draw what is there before you draw what you want. The site had the answer.
