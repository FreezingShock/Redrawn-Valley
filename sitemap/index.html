Long-form notes from Redrawn Valley: heat, shade, soil, sidewalks and walking in the San Fernando Valley, with every number sourced.

/notes/

The hottest day on record in L.A. County happened in Woodland Hills

A 121-degree reading makes a headline. For a site study, the better question is what the thermometer can and cannot tell me about the block I am drawing.

/notes/hottest-corner/

A quarter mile is not the same walk in August

Transit planners measure how far people will walk. In a place that can hit 121 degrees, the better question is how much of that distance is in the sun.

/notes/quarter-mile/

Shade beats paint, and Pacoima shows why

Los Angeles coated 700,000 square feet of asphalt in a reflective surface and measured the result. The numbers are real. They also answer a smaller question than the one I care about.

/notes/shade-beats-paint/

The sidewalk is a promise Los Angeles made in court

A settlement, a $1.4 billion program and three small numbers explain why a sidewalk looks the way it does, and why it is harder to plant than it looks.

/notes/sidewalk-promise/

A shade tree is a soil problem first

Everyone agrees the Valley needs more trees. Almost nobody draws the hole they have to grow in. The arithmetic is simple, and it changes what a street can hold.

/notes/soil-volume/
