How a study is built

Every study has the same four parts

The top bar on a study jumps between them. Reading order matters: the drawing comes after the evidence.

1

Site

What is there now, drawn in plan from aerial imagery, with the constraints that cannot move.

2

Evidence

The problem in numbers, then measured results from other places, then precedents nearby.

3

Design

The proposal drawn in plan, section and detail, with what it costs in parking and space.

4

Delivery

How it could be phased, what to measure before and after, and the list of limits.

Reading conventions

How to read the numbers

Superscript numbers like [1] link to the source list at the bottom of each study. If a number has no source, it is mine, and the study says so.

Every study opens with a box that separates what is real from what is estimated. Dimensions scaled off an aerial photo are estimates, and I give a rough margin.

Drawings are concept sketches, not renderings and not engineered. None of these studies was commissioned by a property owner, and none is advice to build.
