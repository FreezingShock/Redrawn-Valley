A moonlit dark theme, and the circle that switches it on

The site now has a second palette: warm orange-black paper, cream ink, and the brick accent turned into the blue-green of a lit window at night. It is a token swap, so every page, drawing and margin follows it. This is how I chose the colors, checked them, and made the switch feel like drawing a new sheet over the old one.

By Nate AndersonNov. 21, 20266 min read4 sourcesLab 7 of 7

01Why a dark theme

I read this site in a lot of places: a desk lamp, a bus, a bed. Bright cream paper is right for a printed site study and harsh for the last of those. The footer was already a dark band, and it was the part of the page I liked most, so the brief became: make the whole site feel like that band, warm and a little cozy, not cold grey.

The rule I set was that a dark theme must be a change of tokens, not a second stylesheet. Every page already takes its color from variables, so a new set of values should re-color everything at once, including pages other people are editing right now.

02The palette

Paper goes from cream #f6f1e7 to #17120d, a near-black with a clear orange cast, so it reads as a lamp-lit room and not as a screen. The raised surface for cards is a step lighter (#211a13) and the sunk band a step darker (#100c08), the same order as in the light theme. Ink flips to a warm cream, #f3e8d4, which keeps the page from glaring.

  • Paper#17120d
  • Raised#211a13
  • Sunk#100c08
  • Ink#f3e8d4
  • Brick, moonlit#66c3c0
  • Moss, moonlit#9cc3a8
  • Slate, moonlit#86aed2
  • Footer band#0b0806

Brick, the one color that means “do something here,” became #66c3c0, a pale turquoise: what a terracotta wall might look like with only moonlight on it. Moss and slate moved the same way, lighter and a touch cooler, because accents used as text have to rise off a dark page instead of sinking into it.

Solid art fields are a separate set of tokens. A big block of pale turquoise would glare and would not carry the cream linework drawn on it, so the motif tiles use deeper fills (--brick-fill, --moss-fill, --slate-fill) that are identical to the accents in the light theme and muted in the dark one. The footer got its own --band tokens for the same reason: in the dark theme ink becomes cream, and a footer filled with ink would have turned bright.

All 30 pairings I check meet WCAG AA when computed with the standard relative-luminance formula: 4.5 to 1 for text and 3 to 1 for large text and interface lines1. The lowest text pairing is faint caption text on a raised card at 6.6 to 1; the lowest interface line is the input border on a raised card at 3.9 to 1. 03The switch and the circle

There is one button, a hand-drawn sun that turns into a hand-drawn moon, to the left of Search. It is two new icons in the same point-list source as the rest of the set, so they wobble like the others. The phone More sheet has a copy as its first row, with a small drawn switch, and the study pages, which have their own top bar, get it beside their Search link.

The theme comes from the saved choice if there is one, and otherwise from the system setting. A tiny script in the <head> sets it before the page paints, which is why there is no flash of light on a dark page. The choice is saved in the browser and shared across tabs and embedded demos.

When you press the button, the new theme grows out of it as a circle and keeps growing until it covers the page. It uses the View Transitions API2: the browser takes a picture of the old page, the theme changes underneath, and a growing circular clip-path reveals the new one. The radius is the distance to the farthest corner, so the circle always finishes by covering everything, and the duration scales with that distance between 0.7 and 1.1 seconds. Browsers without the API get a disc of the new paper color that grows the same way and then fades to reveal the page. With reduced motion on, the theme swaps instantly.

The art that cannot read a variable is handled by one generated block: CSS data-URI drawings (the hand-drawn rules, wavy band edges and highlighter) are redrawn in dark colors by scripts/build_theme.py, and baked SVG attributes in older drawings are re-mapped to tokens by attribute selectors, which beat presentation attributes.

04The decisions I made

Swap tokens, not stylesheets

Pages being edited by other threads keep working, and a new component gets a dark theme for free if it uses tokens.

CostAnything with a hard-coded color needs a one-time cleanup; I converted what I found and left the rest to the attribute remapping.

Warm near-black, not neutral or blue-black

The footer band the site started with is warm, and a warm black keeps the paper feeling of the light theme.

CostWarm darks are a little less crisp than neutral ones, so I kept text contrast well above the minimum.

Brick becomes moonlit turquoise, everywhere

The accent keeps its job (actions and the current page) and changes only its color, so nothing else needed to change.

CostDrawings that show a red awning now show a turquoise one, and a few sentences in the studies still say red. I left the text alone and flagged it.

The footer stays the darkest thing on the page

It is the reference I started from, so it has its own band tokens and does not follow ink.

CostTwo more token families to maintain.

A circle from the button, not a fade

It ties the change to the thing you pressed and it can be smooth on a long page, because only a picture of the page is animated.

CostNeeds a fallback and a reduced-motion path. I did not build the hand-drawn panel wipe, which was the alternative.

05The palette sheet

Both palettes drawn side by side, whatever theme you are in, with every contrast ratio computed from the hex values as you load it. The button flips the site theme with the circle reveal so you can watch it.

06What I could not verify

Limits of this lab

  • Contrast ratios are computed from flat colors. Text over the hand-drawn art, grain and translucent washes was checked by eye on a laptop and a phone, not measured.
  • I tested the circle reveal in Chromium only. The fallback for browsers without the View Transitions API was written to the same timing but not run in Firefox or Safari.
  • I have not tried the theme with a screen reader or with people. The switch is a real button with a pressed state and a changing label.
  • The theme needs JavaScript. Without it the site stays light.
