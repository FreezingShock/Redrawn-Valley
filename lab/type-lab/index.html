Choosing a hand for the type

Forty-six font entries screened for license, accents, size and legibility, six type directions built from them, and three ways to write text in. The research, the decision (Wobble, now live site-wide) and the live lab are all here.

By Nate AndersonNov. 21, 20267 min read5 sourcesLab 3 of 6

01The question

The site speaks in a serif, sets its interface in a sans, and uses a hand font for eyebrows, tags and notes. I wanted to find out whether a hand-drawn face could carry the site, and which one, without breaking three things I care about: free licenses, Spanish place names with accents, and text that stays readable at caption size on a phone.

02What I screened and what I found

For each candidate I recorded its license, the size of its Latin-subset file, and which of the glyphs I need are missing. The glyph check reads the font file’s own character map for the accented letters in Spanish place names, the degree sign, dashes, the multiplication sign, fractions and superscripts. Sizes are the woff2 files Google Fonts serves for the Latin subset, read on Nov. 21, 2026.1

Almost everything is free for websites. 38 entries are under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, which allows self-hosting and embedding with no fee, and seven are Apache 2.0, which also does.2 The one outlier is the Hershey single-stroke data I use for pen animation, which is free to use with credit.4

Twenty entries made it into at least one direction and three more earned a place as annotation voices. Twenty-three were rejected, and the reasons cluster. Three have no accented letters at all (Nanum Pen Script, Gaegu and Edu SA Beginner), which breaks on a Spanish place name; Gaegu is also missing the degree sign and the dashes. Several are too thin or faint at caption size (Shadows Into Light, Coming Soon, Sue Ellen Francisco). Several are too wide, so headlines wrap (Gloria Hallelujah, Short Stack). Some read as a child’s card or a friendly app and not as a field sketchbook (Indie Flower, Schoolbell, Mali). The sketch-fill fonts turn to noise below 28 pixels. The complete table, with every font, license and verdict, is inside the lab below.

03Six directions

From the survivors I built six directions, each with a full hierarchy on a sample page so I could judge them in context and not as a specimen line.

  1. Field Notebook (Caveat, Kalam, Patrick Hand): looks written on site, and the most personal.
  2. Drafting Sheet: architect lettering for the plans.
  3. Marker and Brush: bold field-sign lettering.
  4. Typewriter Log (Special Elite, Courier Prime): a field log typed up, good where numbers lead.
  5. Hand-cut Serif (Fraunces): a serif with a hand in it.
  6. Wobble (Shantell Sans): one variable font whose letters can move.

Hand fonts are good for titles, labels and notes and poor for long reading. They have small x-heights, uneven rhythm and thin strokes, and none of them read well for more than a few hundred words. That finding shaped every direction: the hand is for the display and the annotation, and the reading text stays a face built for reading.

04The decisions I made

Treat accents as a hard requirement

The Valley has Cañada, Topanga and Tujunga on its maps, and a font that drops the ñ is a font that fails on the first page. Missing glyphs are a rejection, not a footnote.

CostIt removed three otherwise attractive fonts from the shortlist.

Choose Wobble (Shantell Sans) for the whole site

My recommendation in the lab was Hand-cut Serif, with Field Notebook for titles, and I ended up choosing Wobble instead. It is one variable font that covers every role, so the site reads as one hand, and its INFM and BNCE axes let titles be looser while body text stays calmer. IBM Plex Mono stays for citations, captions and numbers, so evidence still looks like evidence. The font tokens now all point at Shantell Sans.

CostHand fonts read slower over long text, which is why the body setting is deliberately calm. At about 170 KB with all axes it is the heaviest option, trimming axes would cut it, and Shantell has no italic file, so italics are synthesized.

Add three writing effects with two speeds each

A pen write-on from single-stroke Hershey data, a typewriter that strikes keys at irregular times, and a sweep that works on any font. Each has a fast and a slow speed, and under reduced motion nothing plays by itself, though a button you press still plays.

CostThe pen effect needs its own letterform data, so accents there are added by code and are approximate.

Keep the lab as the record, marked noindex

The lab stays on the site as the candidate sheet behind the choice, so the research and the five directions I did not pick remain visible. It is noindex because it is a working page; this article is the readable version.

CostThe lab shows six directions but only one is live, so the lab no longer matches the site.

06What I could not verify

Limits of this lab

  • I did not read every license file. I used the license Google Fonts lists for each family and cross-checked it against Fontsource, which agreed for every font here.
  • Glyph coverage was tested on the default weight, not every weight. Playwrite’s download size could not be measured from the CSS API.
  • I judged legibility by eye at 375 and 1280 pixels, not with readers, so every “rejected” note is my opinion. I have not tested the calmer Wobble body setting with readers either.
