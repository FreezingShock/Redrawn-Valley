How the drawings gallery finds every figure

The studies and notes hold 24 labeled drawings spread over 8 pages. A short script finds them, and one page draws them side by side, so a new figure shows up without anyone editing a list.

By Nate AndersonNov. 21, 20265 min read2 sourcesLab 6

01Why the drawings needed one sheet

The drawings are the part of this site I care about most, and they were hidden in long pages. To compare the Topanga plan with the Fallbrook plan you had to open two studies and scroll. I wanted one place where every plan, section, sketch and chart sits next to the others at the same size.

I did not want a second list to maintain. A hand-kept gallery goes stale the first time I add a figure and forget to add its entry. So the rule became: if a figure is described for screen readers, it is in the gallery.

02How a figure gets found

A figure counts if it is an inline SVG inside the page body, has role="img" and an aria-label, is at least 360 units wide, and is not marked aria-hidden. I borrowed that test from the zoom button I had already built, so anything you can zoom on a page is something the gallery lists. Banners, infobox sketches and icons are decorative and hidden from screen readers, so they never appear.

scripts/build_drawings.py walks the studies, notes and field guide, applies the test, and writes only metadata: the page, the order of the figure on it, its description, its caption, the nearest section heading and the stages of any staged plan. That file is small (about 14 KB for 24 drawings) because it holds no artwork.

The gallery page then loads the source page and lifts the figure out of it. Each drawing is placed in a shadow root along with the stylesheets of the page it came from, which keeps the Topanga linework looking like Topanga and the notes charts looking like notes charts, even though their class names collide. Cards load as they scroll near the screen.

Type and topic are guessed. The type comes from words in the description, then the caption (a plan, a section, a perspective, a chart, otherwise a diagram). The topic uses the same topic slugs as the topic hubs, limited to the topics the page is already tagged with. A figure can override either guess with data-fig-type or data-fig-topics.

03The decisions I made

Store metadata, draw from the source page

Copying the SVG into the data file would have been faster to load, but the Topanga study alone is over 500 KB of markup and every copy would drift from its page. Drawing from the source means a fix to a figure shows up in the gallery at once.

CostThe gallery fetches each source page it needs, so the first visit downloads more than a thumbnail list would.

Use a shadow root for each drawing

The studies style their figures with generated class names that differ from page to page. A shadow root keeps each page’s styles with its own figure, so nothing needed renaming. Browsers document this isolation in MDN’s guide to shadow DOM.1

CostEach card loads its own copy of the page stylesheets, which the browser caches but still has to parse.

Reuse the zoom lightbox, add three controls

The lightbox already handled pinch, drag and keyboard zoom, so I added previous and next, a stage stepper for staged plans, and a “View on page” link instead of building another viewer.

CostThe gallery now depends on one function in interact.js that accepts a prebuilt element.

Filters that show counts and keep state in the address

Every chip shows how many drawings it would leave, so you never click into an empty result. The filters live in the URL, and #drawing-id opens one drawing, so a drawing can be linked to.

CostCounts are recalculated on every change, which is fine at 24 drawings and would need thought at a few thousand.

Load cards as they near the screen

Only cards close to the viewport fetch and draw, using an intersection observer.2 Cards ease in once, and under reduced motion they simply appear.

CostFast scrolling can outrun the loading, so a card may show a hatched placeholder for a moment.

05What I could not verify

Limits of this lab

  • Type and topic are keyword guesses and I checked them by eye on the 24 drawings I have. A new figure with unusual wording could be mislabeled until I set data-fig-type.
  • The staged-plan stepper depends on how the study pages mark their steps. A new page that stages a figure in another way would show only its last stage.
  • I tested in a current Chromium browser and at 375 and 1280 pixels wide. I did not test Safari or Firefox, and shadow roots with linked stylesheets are the part I would check first.
  • The gallery needs JavaScript. Without it, a link sends you to the studies and notes.
